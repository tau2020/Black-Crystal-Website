An Environmental & Social Impact Assessment (ESIA) identifies how a proposed project could affect the natural environment and the people who live and work around it, and sets out practical measures to avoid, minimise, restore or offset those effects before decisions are made.

Black Crystal has prepared ESIAs, EIAs, Environmental Prospectuses, scoping reports, addenda and Environmental Management Plans across mining, energy, infrastructure and tourism. Our 2024 company profile records assessments prepared to International Finance Corporation (IFC) standards for platinum mining, renewable energy and power-line projects.

## What an ESIA typically involves

- Screening and scoping to agree the issues, study area and specialist inputs that matter for the project
- Baseline studies of the physical, biological, social and cultural environment
- Stakeholder engagement and public consultation
- Identification of impacts and assessment of their significance, including cumulative effects
- Mitigation measures and an Environmental and Social Management Plan (ESMP) with monitoring commitments
- Reporting in the format required by the competent authority and, where applicable, by lenders

## Why it matters

For many significant developments an approved assessment is a precondition for authorisation, and for project finance it is often a condition of lending. A well-scoped ESIA also reduces the risk of late redesigns, community conflict and delays by bringing environmental and social constraints into the design early.

## Standards and frameworks

In Zimbabwe, environmental impact assessment is governed by the Environmental Management Act [Chapter 20:27] and administered by the Environmental Management Agency (EMA). Where projects seek international finance, assessments can be structured to address the IFC Performance Standards and the Equator Principles. Requirements differ between jurisdictions and projects, so the applicable framework is confirmed at the start of every assignment.
