When a project needs land, people's homes, fields, grazing and livelihoods can be affected, sometimes permanently. How displacement is handled often determines whether a project earns lasting community support or inherits lasting grievance.

Black Crystal's 2024 company profile records several assignments in this area: Relocation Action Plans as part of the ESIAs for the proposed Batoka Gorge Hydro-Electric Scheme (2018) and the 2nd Alaska–Sherwood 400/330 kV transmission line (2017–2018); a Displacement Impact Verification Study for a platinum mine solar project (2023–2024); and reviews using the Socio-Economic Assessment Toolbox (SEAT), including a Relocation Action Plan review, for a platinum mine (2014–2016).

## Start with avoidance

The most effective resettlement plan is the one that is not needed. Alternative alignments for power lines, adjusted layouts for solar plants and careful siting of infrastructure can significantly reduce the number of affected households.

## Understand who is affected, and how

A socio-economic baseline records households, assets, land use and livelihoods before a project begins. It should capture people who use land informally as well as formal owners, and identify vulnerable groups who may need additional support.

## Plan relocation with people, not for them

Relocation planning should set out eligibility, compensation and livelihood restoration measures, timelines and responsibilities, and a grievance procedure that affected people can actually use. Meaningful consultation is essential at every stage.

## Verify what happened

Verification studies and reviews check whether displacement impacts have been correctly identified and addressed. Tools such as SEAT help operations understand their social and economic effects over time and track whether commitments are being met.

## Principles to hold on to

- Avoid and minimise displacement first
- Base decisions on a robust socio-economic baseline
- Restore livelihoods, not just structures
- Keep engagement and grievance channels open throughout

Projects seeking international finance are commonly expected to meet lender standards on land acquisition and involuntary resettlement, such as IFC Performance Standard 5.
