Forest carbon projects promise something rare: a way for standing forests to generate income for the communities who live alongside them. But the credibility of every carbon credit rests on measurements made in the field.

In 2010 Black Crystal was appointed as technical advisors to a certifiable carbon reduction project in Zimbabwe. According to our 2024 company profile, the project aimed to conserve forested areas and associated wildlife habitats in four Communal Lands in northern Zimbabwe, with Carbon Green Africa supporting a series of projects to uplift livelihoods and support the self-empowerment of local communities. Revenue from carbon credits generated through the REDD+ project was expected to be an important source of income for those communities.

## The work behind the numbers

Black Crystal was largely responsible for:

- vegetation biomass and soil carbon fieldwork;
- developing field protocols for data collection; and
- checking, computerising and carrying out the preliminary analysis of vegetation and soil data.

These tasks are unglamorous, but they decide whether a project's carbon estimates can withstand independent scrutiny. Consistent protocols mean that plots measured by different teams, in different seasons, can be compared. Careful data checking catches errors before they flow into carbon calculations.

## Validation

The profile records that the project received Climate Community Biodiversity Standard validation in February 2012 and Voluntary Carbon Standard validation in September 2012, and describes it as only the second such project in Africa and one of the largest in the world.

## Why it matters beyond carbon

REDD+ (reducing emissions from deforestation and forest degradation) links climate goals with biodiversity conservation and community development. Projects that combine robust measurement with genuine community benefit help show that conservation can be part of rural livelihoods rather than a constraint on them.

## Lessons for practitioners

- Invest in field protocols before the first plot is measured
- Treat data quality control as a core deliverable, not an afterthought
- Integrate biodiversity and community considerations alongside carbon
- Plan for independent validation from the start
