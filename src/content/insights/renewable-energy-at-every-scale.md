Renewable energy is central to a lower-carbon future, but "renewable" does not mean "impact-free". Solar farms occupy land, wind turbines interact with birds and landscapes, and hydro-electric schemes transform rivers. Good environmental assessment helps renewable projects deliver their benefits without creating avoidable new problems.

Black Crystal's 2024 company profile records energy assessments spanning photovoltaic facilities from 150 kW to 300 MW, a wind project in Guruve, a Renewable Energy Technology Park in Chipinge, the proposed Batoka Gorge Hydro-Electric Scheme on the Zambezi River (with Environmental Resources Management) and a hydroelectric power scheme in Masvingo Province.

## Solar: land, water and access

Utility-scale solar requires significant land. Key questions include what the land is currently used for, whether vegetation clearing affects sensitive habitats, how stormwater and erosion will be managed, and how panels will be cleaned where water is scarce. Smaller facilities usually raise fewer issues, and the assessment should be proportionate: an Environmental Prospectus and Environmental Management Plan may be appropriate where a full ESIA is not.

## Wind: birds, bats and landscape

Wind projects raise questions about collision risks for birds and bats, noise, shadow flicker and visual change in valued landscapes. Pre-construction surveys and careful turbine siting are among the most effective forms of mitigation.

## Hydro-electric schemes: rivers and people

Dams and reservoirs can alter river flows, sediment and fisheries far downstream, and may require the resettlement of households. The ESIA and Relocation Action Plan recorded in our profile for the Batoka Gorge scheme reflect how social planning sits alongside environmental assessment on projects of this kind.

## Finance and standards

Several renewable energy assessments in the profile were prepared to International Finance Corporation (IFC) standards, including the Chipinge Renewable Energy Technology Park, the Guruve wind project and a 300 MW solar PV facility for Karo Power. Where projects seek international finance, aligning the assessment with lender requirements from the outset avoids costly gap-filling later; the profile also records an IFC Performance Standards gap review and scope of work for a 100 MW solar facility.

## Key takeaways

- Match the depth of assessment to the scale and sensitivity of the project
- Survey early: siting is the most powerful mitigation
- Treat resettlement and livelihoods as core issues on large schemes
- Align with lender standards from the start where finance is involved
